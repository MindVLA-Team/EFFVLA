Abstract

Vision-Language-Action models combine a pretrained vision encoder, a language backbone, and an action head, but the relative contribution of these design choices is difficult to establish across systems. We fix the backbone families, SigLIP2 and Qwen2.5, and the training pipeline while sweeping action-head design and module scale, pairing the efficiency comparisons with measured on-device latency.

The study yields three findings. Copying the backbone's last transformer layers into the L1 head gives the largest positive effect in our matched comparisons. After this initialization, the action head is the highest-return module to scale. Returns then diminish at a modest size within the tested regime. Attention analysis is consistent with a backbone-compatibility interpretation but does not establish its cause.

These findings specify EffVLA, a compact model with competitive standard LIBERO performance and strong results on most LIBERO-Plus perturbation axes. We also evaluate the recipe on SimplerEnv and a real SO-ARM101 arm.

Three findings

1 · What to build

Initialize from the backbone

Within matched L1 heads, VLM initialization raises success from 72.7% to 79.8%. The inference computation is unchanged; measured latency is 39.3 vs 39.2 ms.

2 · Where to scale

Scale the initialized action head

From s to l, action-head scaling adds 9.4 points for 2.4 ms, about 4 points/ms. The matched random L1 head gains 3.1 points.

3 · How big

Returns diminish beyond l

None of EffVLA's tested g-module probes improves its aggregate LIBERO-Plus score over l. This is an observation within the tested family and benchmark.

Action-head design

We study 63 configurations under a shared DROID pretraining and LIBERO fine-tuning pipeline. The four action-head axes are decoder, loss, initialization, and inference passes. L1 initialization pairs share architecture and module sizes.

Readout interfaces differ across head families: transformer heads use KV sharing, OFT reads hidden states, and FAST decodes through the language backbone. The random flow-matching head uses DiT, whereas the VLM-initialized flow-matching head uses plain transformer blocks.

Attention and backbone compatibility

On the black-bowl rollout, the initialized head assigns more attention mass to the instruction: 0.223 vs 0.030. Its visual attention overlaps the bowl, but its strongest text tokens are assistant and put. The role token may act as an attention sink or prompt-boundary marker.

Across 128 rollouts, 64 per condition, mean instruction attention mass is 0.233 vs 0.055. Aggregate instruction-word rankings emphasize task nouns, while total text mass also includes template tokens.

Additional weight-space analysis in the arXiv version

Weight-Gram CKA measures projection-weight structure, not input-conditioned activations. During fine-tuning, the initialized trajectory decreases from about 0.88 to 0.76, compared with 0.27 to 0.24 for random initialization. Layerwise values are approximately 0.72–0.81 and 0.21–0.27, respectively.

The trajectory uses a fixed pretrained-backbone reference; the layerwise panel uses each model's final fine-tuned backbone. The six success-rate rows reuse the l/l/l anchor and represent five unique configurations. These measurements support preservation of weight structure, not a demonstrated semantic or causal mechanism.

Module scaling and saturation

VLM initialization increases the return to action-head capacity: s-to-l scaling adds 9.4 points, compared with 3.1 for the matched random L1 head and 4.7 within the random-initialized flow-matching sweep. These are capacity effects, distinct from the initialization comparison.

EffVLA and benchmark results

EffVLA combines a VLM-initialized transformer head, KV sharing, and single-pass L1 regression with SigLIP2 and Qwen2.5-3B. It achieves 98.2% on LIBERO and 79.8% on LIBERO-Plus, with 39.2 ms model inference per action chunk.
